Samy's Number Names Generator :3

Every number past a thousand gets a name built from Latin roots. Type one in below, no matter how large. Right now this is v0.3.6 of the website.

Accepts a plain number, scientific notation (like 4.2e45), or a power of ten (like 10^33 or 2.7*10^887).
Works for full names or their short forms, like "vigintillion" or just "Vg".

Drawer I: thousand to nonillion

The common ones. 1e3 through 1e30, each with its own irregular Latin root.

Drawer II: decillion to nonagintillion

The main tens-roots from 1e33 onward. Everything in between reads additively: a tens-root plus a whole Drawer I word, like vigintitrillion (1e72), which is viginti (twenty) plus trillion, rather than mashing three prefixes into one. The lookup tool above will name any of them.

Drawer III: centillion to novemcentillion

Past 1e303, the system adds a hundreds prefix. Shown here in steps of one hundred. This construction is deliberately additive rather than a straight prefix mash-up, which is what lets 103 (centitrillion) and 300 (trecentillion) stay clearly distinct instead of colliding into the same word.

Drawer IV: millinillion and the recursive extension

The base dictionary runs out around 1e3000 (n = 999), but the pattern doesn't have to. "Milli" and "nilli" are dedicated connector words, never used as a real number on their own, so they can chain onto each block of the additive naming above without ever being confused for part of the number itself. That's what lets this fold back on itself indefinitely. The lookup tool above will name anything in this range too, no matter how far you push it.

The base naming (from the thousands through the hundreds tier) is built additively, following the construction that Sbiis Saibian laid out in his Extensible Illion System, specifically to avoid the kind of naming collision (like 103 and 300 both wanting to be "trecentillion") that straight Latin-prefix mashing runs into. Past that base range, numbers are named using a dedicated, never-reused "milli/nilli" connector pattern that chains indefinitely without new collisions, verified by round-trip testing across the full base range plus a wide sample of much larger numbers. Abbreviations shown here follow a separate, consistent shorthand scheme of my own, not tied to either naming convention. Right now I'm working on my own system to remove the milli-nilli-nilli-nilli... chain for arbitrarily large numbers, but making it 100% functional and stable is pretty hard, so it might take a while.
