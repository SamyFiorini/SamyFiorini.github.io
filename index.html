Accepts a plain number, scientific notation (like 4.2e45), or powers of ten (like 10^33 or 8.8*10^734)

Drawer I: the ones

The common ones. 1e3 through 1e30, each with its own irregular Latin root

Drawer II: the tens

The main tens roots from 1e33 onward. Everything in between reads additively: a tens root plus a whole Drawer I word, like vigintitrillion (1e72), which is viginti (twenty) plus trillion

Drawer III: the hundreds

Starting at 1e303 (n=100), the system adds a hundreds prefix. This construction is deliberately additive rather than a straight prefix mash-up, which is what lets 103 (centitrillion) and 300 (trecentillion) stay clearly distinct instead of having the same name

Drawer IV: the blocks

Starting at 1e3003 (n = 1000), the naming keeps going by reusing ten dedicated "block" words: myri, million, billion... nonillion. Each one a thousand times bigger than the last. So the thousandth illion is myriillion, the millionth is millionillion, the billionth is billionillion... all the way to the nonillionth illion. Past that, a second dedicated pair, "vasti" and "nulla", takes over and chains indefinitely

Drawer V: the repetition

Once the ten block words above are exhausted, "vasti" and "nulla" chain the whole system into itself indefinitely. The lookup tool above does have a hard cap on how big of a number you can name, but theoretically you can name any number

This naming system started from Conway-Guy-Wechsler and Sbiis Saibian's Extensible Illion System. From there it's been substantially extended and modified. Every layer was checked by generating tens of thousands of names and parsing them back to confirm no two different numbers ever produce the same word. Abbreviations shown here follow a separate, consistent shorthand of my own.
